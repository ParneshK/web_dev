Fun Space Fact

The Sun is so big that one million Earths could fit inside it.

A day on Venus is longer than an entire year on Venus.

Saturn has a lower density than water, meaning the ringed giant would float if you could find a bathtub big enough.

Olympus Mons on Mars is a giant volcano nearly three times the height of Mount Everest.

It rains solid diamonds deep inside the extreme atmospheres of Neptune and Uranus.

Astronaut footprints on the Moon will stay there for millions of years because there is no atmosphere or wind to erase them.

Jupiter's Great Red Spot is a colossal storm that has been raging for hundreds of years and is large enough to fit Earth inside it.

Uranus orbits the Sun lying completely on its side, rolling around its orbit like a tumbling ball.

Despite being closest to the Sun, Mercury is not the hottest planet because Venus traps heat with a thick atmosphere.

Jupiter's moon Io is the most volcanically active world in the solar system, featuring hundreds of active sulfur volcanoes.

Comet tails always point directly away from the Sun, regardless of the direction the comet itself is moving.
